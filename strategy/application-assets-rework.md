### Case structure alongside STAR

**Additional recollection, 2026-09-23:** Manel is certain Cyrièle explicitly asked for problem framing at the start of the interview. He tentatively recalls exploration, solution, and outcomes as the other stages. Record only problem framing as certain in his recollection; the complete sequence is not a verified quotation or employer rubric.

**Proposed deck structure:** Use the following four sections as an editorial choice, supported by this recollection. Keep personal responsibility and shared ownership clear throughout.

1. **Problem framing:** Who was affected, what were they trying to do, what evidence showed the problem, why did it matter, and what scope, constraints, and success criteria guided the work? Do not substitute a company introduction or feature description for the problem.
2. **Exploration:** Which options did you consider, what did you learn from research or testing, and how did that evidence change the direction? Select the decisions needed to understand the solution; do not inventory every activity.
3. **Solution:** Show how the chosen approach addresses the framed problem, why it was chosen, the main tradeoff, and your contribution. Use a concrete flow or example to connect the design to the original difficulty.
4. **Outcomes:** State what shipped and changed, what evidence supports it, what remains unmeasured, and what you learned. Distinguish delivered outputs from user or business outcomes; never invent results to complete the structure.

Use STAR for concise behavioural answers and individual decision stories within the case. The approximate mapping is problem framing plus responsibility → Situation/Task; exploration and solution decisions → Action; supported outcomes → Result. The deck can use the four case headings without repeating a complete STAR sequence on every slide. Follow a future interviewer's requested structure when provided.
