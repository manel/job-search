**Audit date: 23 September 2026.** Sources: Career OS application records, the read-only job-search ledger and saved CVs, the live portfolio, and the experience register. This measures the recorded search, not every application that might exist outside these records. Employer statuses are the latest recorded confirmations, not a fresh inbox audit.

## Assessment

The observable bottleneck is reaching a first conversation. The evidence does **not** justify repeatedly rebuilding the whole application package. There are identifiable delivery and presentation defects to fix, but little causal feedback explaining individual rejections. Changing targeting, salary, CV, portfolio, and outreach together prevents learning which changes help.

My strongest hypothesis is that the package makes relevant evidence too expensive to find. Historical unreadable PDFs are a separate, confirmed defect. The portfolio has substantial product judgment, research, collaboration, and operational leadership; it often reveals those after long introductions and technical exposition. This is a presentation diagnosis, not a demonstrated rejection cause.

## Targeting and interpretation

Some earlier assessments were more confident than their evidence justified. The legacy CloudPay notes call salary the likely screening trigger despite generic rejection wording; the old Bitpanda notes speculate about an ATS history cut. Those are hypotheses, not findings, and the newer canonical records correctly remove that certainty. Reworking assets around such explanations risks solving an imagined problem.

Historical “Proceed” decisions also sometimes rewarded shared domain language more than the rare requirement. 3Commas explicitly wanted deep trading knowledge and included management scope; Fluidra's systems role was mobile-focused; the current AirHelp role includes substantial go-to-market collateral. Those gaps were recorded, and some applications were deliberate overrides. They should remain visible as higher-risk bets, not enter a later analytics cohort labelled simply “strong fit.” None is a proven rejection cause.

Conversely, the old sourcing rules sometimes dismiss an industry before examining the actual user problem. The stronger filter is whether the work involves professional workflows, complex decisions, research, architecture, and the IC scope Manel wants. Preserve genuine-interest and location constraints, but avoid assuming a consumer-facing company has no relevant internal platform role. Be stricter about unsupported hard requirements and more precise about the job's actual work.

For future triage, record one sentence each for the central hiring problem, the direct proof, and the decisive gap. “B2B + AI + design systems” is an overlap description, not a sufficient hiring argument. Keep an override flag so an intentional stretch does not contaminate the comparison with direct-evidence applications.

No font resources were found in those files. This is a real avoidable readability problem. Whether any employer used OCR or rejected because of it is unknown. Today's archived files may not be identical to the uploaded bytes.

The other exports are not all equivalent. Nineteen of the 32 use Type 3 fonts and yield text; they must not be described as wholly unreadable. Three recent files, AirHelp, Datavant 2, and Moniepoint, use embedded Inter fonts and yield text. The latest AirHelp and Datavant files are about 23 KiB each. The zero-text problem therefore appears fixed in those saved recent versions, but the upload itself was not inspected.

Remaining defects and improvements:

1. **No explicit link annotations in any of the 32 PDFs.** Some viewers may auto-detect visible URLs. Add actual portfolio and case-study hyperlinks to future exports, then test them. The latest CVs still expose only the homepage URL, although accompanying application materials sometimes link a relevant case.
2. **The AirHelp and Datavant 2 PDFs start page two with the final Abacum bullet.** Keep a role's bullets together where practical, or repeat an Abacum continuation label. AirHelp's two pages render cleanly, but this break weakens scanning.
3. **Extraction order still needs attention.** In pypdf, the recent CV skills category labels appear after their descriptions, and sidebar contact details appear after experience. That is an extraction-order issue, not evidence of a visibly broken layout or failure in every ATS. Prefer a reliable single reading sequence and validate both text extraction and page rendering.
4. **The saved Biorce CV headline is “Senior Design Systems Designer,” although the role was Senior Product Designer with AI/research emphasis.** Check the final PDF against the intended role, not just its source Markdown. This file still converted to a screen, so the mismatch cannot be its rejection explanation.
5. **The old Datavant record says the submitted PDF had reversed pages, but the current archived file renders in normal order.** Preserve the confirmed historical account; do not assert that today's archive reproduces the original send. Save exact uploaded bytes prospectively.
6. **Review “15 years designing complex B2B products.”** The visible employment history includes a decade of varied studio work. A more precise future summary would distinguish total design experience from recent B2B specialization. This is a wording recommendation, not a challenge to the years of experience.
7. **For product roles, lead with what users can now do and the consequential design decision.** Pipeline tooling is useful supporting evidence, but naming CI checks, drift detection, and output formats before the relevant product result can obscure the hiring fit. For systems roles, keep those technical details and add adoption, governance, accessibility, and component-behavior proof where supported.

Do not resend every old application or overwrite historical materials. Use the corrected export workflow for new sends and verified follow-ups.

### Signals to strengthen, not fabricate

- **Product strategy:** show one decision about where the product should go, the alternatives, the constraint, who agreed or disagreed, and the chosen sequence. You already have pieces in the cases; make the chain visible. Factorial's feedback supports practicing this for zero-to-one challenges, not concluding that every rejection reflects missing strategic ability.
- **Research-to-design traceability:** put an observed user misunderstanding beside the UI change it caused and what testing did or did not establish. Dataset Manager is the strongest starting point. More research-process vocabulary would add little.
- **Interaction and visual craft:** a readable task sequence with empty, invalid, dependency, loading, and success states communicates more than another broad component montage. Only show states actually designed, or explicitly label new proposals.
- **Accessibility:** the canonical evidence does not establish specific accessibility work. For systems hiring this is a useful gap to investigate. Recover real keyboard, focus, contrast, error, or assistive-technology decisions if they exist; otherwise build a clearly labelled present-day demonstration. Do not claim past WCAG compliance.
- **Leadership beyond sole ownership:** Abax's funding, weekly decisions, migration ownership, and contribution model are stronger than “sole designer” alone. Mentoring or organizational impact needs actual examples before making stronger Staff claims.
- **Measurement:** missing instrumentation limits commercial-outcome claims. Recover qualified operational signals or source artifacts where possible; avoid inventing percentages to make a case look senior. A before-redesign customer complaint establishes the problem, not a measured post-redesign improvement.

## Visual improvements, in order

1. **Bring work into the first screen.** At 390 px, the first homepage project begins roughly 1,157 px below the page top; Dataset Manager's first image begins around 1,191 px. The long lead text delays both proof and craft. Reduce introductory copy and top spacing before changing the aesthetic.
2. **Use a compact case opening:** title, one-sentence user change, role/team/timeframe, one principal decision, outcome with evidence qualifier, then a readable hero. Put the long background below it. Target an opening a reviewer can explain after one minute.
3. **Make key images explanatory.** Use tight crops at readable scale and two or three brief callouts tied to decisions. Offer enlargement for dense tables or component boards. Keep an overview when needed, then reveal the detail. Do not scale a full Figma canvas down until labels disappear.
4. **Replace some prose with matched before/after evidence.** Same task, same user, comparable screen crop, and a short caption explaining what changed. For Abax/Remuner, show the system applied in a real workflow as well as the library.
5. **Repair the mobile navigation.** At 390 px the name and Selected Work wrap awkwardly; the case navigation is crowded. The homepage reports 411 px of document width in a 390 px viewport; the sampled Dataset page does not. Use a compact name + Work + Contact row and move secondary links to an accessible menu. Verify the source of the homepage overflow before implementing a fix.
6. **Increase caption legibility and preserve comfortable text widths.** The local case styles use 12 px captions at 0.55 opacity and let overview paragraphs span the full content width. Use ordinary readable contrast, approximately 14 px captions, and a roughly 60–75-character reading measure. These are proposed design settings, not an accessibility certification.
7. **Add navigation within long cases.** The desktop sidebar links between projects; a short Problem / Decisions / Result index would make the evidence easier to locate within a project. Keep the essential summary visible without requiring interaction.

The current restrained style is coherent. A decorative rebrand or more animation has lower priority than evidence hierarchy, readable screenshots, and credible outcome wording.

## A bounded next experiment

**First, repair defects once:** export QA, direct clickable case links, the Abax denominator, qualified outcome language, mobile navigation, and shorter openings. Archive a dated portfolio version and the exact PDF for every future application.

**Then test the package with five relevant reviewers before rewriting all cases.** Ask them to inspect it for 60 seconds and answer: What role would you hire this person for? Which decision demonstrates senior judgment? What changed for users? Which claim needs clarification? Then let them inspect one case in depth. Repeated inability to find existing evidence is a presentation problem; repeated demand for evidence that does not exist is an experience/proof gap. These reviews diagnose comprehension, not interview-conversion probability.

**Use the next 10 well-matched submitted applications as a monitored cohort.** Prefer Senior IC complex-workflow roles, where the direct evidence is strongest. Include systems roles where the actual mandate fits; count that lane separately and reserve judgment until at least five updated-case submissions have had time to respond. Staff/Lead remains selective and should be justified by the role's actual cross-team scope. Do not wait to accumulate ten perfect vacancies, or hold a ready application while endlessly editing assets.

For each application record: exact job ID, central must-have and supporting case, location/employment eligibility, scope, salary asked/stated/published band, direct/referral/outreach path, exact PDF hash, extractability/link QA, portfolio version and case URL, submission date, first human response, interview dates, and employer decision/reason. Acknowledgments and portal status are not human conversations.

Review at 14 and 35 days from each submission; compare applications at the same elapsed age. Keep explicit rejection, vacancy closure, and silence separate. Record substantive manual replies separately from recruiter screens. Primary outcome: submitted → first recruiter/hiring conversation. Secondary outcome: screen → next round. If several things change together, call this a revised-package cohort, not an A/B test attributing uplift to one change.

Keep the revised assets stable through the cohort unless a factual or functional defect appears. Revisit targeting if clear rare requirements remain unsupported; revisit presentation if reviewers cannot find the proof; revisit interview framing if losses happen after portfolio/challenge rounds. No salary reduction is justified solely by generic rejections. The new cohort is still too small to support precise causal estimates.
