## Remuner foundations — confirmed activities

Manel clarified on 2026-10-06 that he established:

- **Project/folder structure:** Organized Figma files by role or scope: Admin, IC/Manager, app-wide Core projects, and Libraries. Each file separated exploration from implementation, with dated project pages and iteration identifiers in both sections. Manel describes the naming convention as `MMYYYY -> Project name (iteration #)`; inspected pages use month/year labels such as `1025`, and implementation sections are labelled Handoff. File-level authoring conventions are now established. Manel confirmed use by himself, engineers, the wider team, the Head of Product, and a designer who joined later. Prior conditions and the effect on finding/reusing/reviewing work remain unknown. The later designer’s join date and overlap with Manel are not established.
- **UX principles:** Addressed choice overload through progressive disclosure and the goal of reducing cognitive load; Manel corrected “cognitive law” to “cognitive load”. He describes a shift from displaying all available capabilities to placing actions where users needed them. In the IC dashboard, the team reduced click options and interactions needed to discover information and put other information behind specific buttons. This is a concrete application of the principles, not a measured usability outcome. Exact elements, iteration, collaborators, release status and feedback remain open. Keep it separate from the manager-view redesign and its recorded mixed feedback.
- **Rituals:** Introduced weekly planning with the CEO, CTO, CFO and himself, plus ad hoc refinement mainly for small projects with the CTO and a frontend engineer. Planning covered product planning, new features, user feedback and current-project status. Refinement reviewed design feasibility, improvement ideas and changes before handoff. Formats, participants, cadence and purposes are user-confirmed. Facilitation, specific decisions and outcomes remain unspecified; refinement must not be described as weekly or as a design-team critique.

All three foundations now have concrete user-confirmed examples: shared file organization, principles applied to the IC dashboard, and weekly planning/ad hoc refinement. Specific decisions and facilitation responsibilities for the rituals, and outcomes for all three, remain open. Follow-up should recover specific decisions and feedback rather than assign generic process benefits.
