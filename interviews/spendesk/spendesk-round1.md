---

# 1. What this interview is actually testing

This is not a portfolio interview yet.

The recruiter is mainly trying to establish:

1. Is your experience relevant to the role?
2. Are you operating at the right level?
3. Why Spendesk?
4. Why are you looking to move?
5. Are your location/work preferences compatible?
6. Are your salary expectations compatible?
7. Are there any obvious concerns before introducing you to the hiring team?

The objective is **not to prove that you're the best designer they've ever seen**.

The objective is to leave the recruiter thinking:

> "This person's background is highly relevant, they communicate well, they're motivated by the right things, and I should move them forward."

# 2. Spendesk — company context

## What Spendesk does

Spendesk is a European B2B spend-management platform.

The product covers areas including:

- Corporate cards
- Expenses
- Invoices / accounts payable
- Procurement
- Approvals
- Budgets
- Reporting
- Accounting / financial operations

The core value proposition is roughly:

> Give employees autonomy to spend while giving finance teams visibility, control and compliance.

Spendesk currently reports:

- 5,000+ companies using the platform
- €10B+ in spend processed
- Operations across 35+ countries
- Profitability achieved in 2025

The company is therefore operating at meaningful scale rather than being an early-stage startup still searching for product-market fit.

## Problem

## Key framing

Don't overclaim.

> "I tend to use research pragmatically. The method depends on the uncertainty we're trying to resolve. That can mean customer interviews, structured usability testing, looking at support or customer-call evidence, or competitor research.
>
> I try to make research answer a specific product question rather than treating it as a separate phase of the design process."

Then use the formula language example:

> "For example, the formula syntax work at Abacum combined competitor research with evidence from customer calls before we made the language decisions."

# 21. "How do you deal with ambiguity?"

Use Dataset Manager or AI.

Suggested structure:

> "I try to make the ambiguity explicit first: what do we actually know, what are we assuming, and what decisions need to be made?

>
> At Abacum, for example, the problem with data configuration wasn't simply that the UI was difficult. The deeper issue was that we were exposing the underlying technical model to finance users. That changed the direction of the solution."

Excellent senior-level answer.
