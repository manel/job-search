## Situational questions

The employer guide also describes hypothetical scenarios. Keep proposed actions in the future or conditional tense. Do not pass them off as past experience.

**A provider workflow generates many support requests. Where would you start?**

Clarify which users, task, and requests are involved. Look at support evidence and observe the workflow with users where possible. Compare user evidence with internal-team accounts. Identify whether the obstacle is understanding, missing information, permissions, product behaviour, or a process constraint. Choose a small improvement with Product and Engineering, test it, and agree how to assess it. Possible future measures include task completion without help, time to completion, errors, and support contacts per completed task. These are proposed measures, not known Datavant baselines.

**A PM needs delivery this week, but research reveals a serious issue.**

Clarify the consequence and evidence. Separate a cosmetic concern from a task failure or material risk. Bring a minimum safe option and its tradeoffs to the PM and Engineering; agree what must change now, what can be deferred, and what evidence would trigger reconsideration. If asked for a real example, switch to a documented episode rather than inventing a PM dispute.

**Different products use different patterns. Would you standardise them?**

First check whether the user intent and constraints are actually the same. Reuse a common pattern where it helps users and teams, allow justified differences, and plan migration with the owners. Abax supports how you have handled shared adoption, but is not evidence of consolidating five separate products.

### Additional scenarios: decisions under pressure

Added from the follower-collected LinkedIn list supplied by Manel. These are practice prompts adapted to an in-house product team, not verified Deloitte or predicted Datavant questions. Use reasoning cues, not memorised answers.

**A stakeholder reverses an approved decision, or the designs no longer match the requirements. What do you do?**

Establish what changed: new evidence, a business constraint, an outdated requirement, or a preference. Compare the intended user task, current designs, and latest agreed scope with the PM and Engineering. Explain the consequences of the available options for users, delivery, and rework. Agree who makes the decision, update the source of truth, and make the change visible to everyone affected. Prior approval is useful context, not a reason to ignore new evidence.

**You cannot get budget or access for user testing. How would you validate the design?**

Identify the riskiest assumption first. Seek a smaller way to test it: a brief session through an existing customer contact, observation, or a focused prototype task. Use support records, existing research, and product data to inform the decision. Internal experts and heuristic reviews can expose problems, but they do not establish that users understand the design. State what remains unknown and agree how to limit exposure and gather evidence after a staged release if direct testing remains unavailable.

**An accessibility problem appears shortly before handoff. How do you respond?**

Reproduce the issue with QA and Engineering, identify who is affected, and check whether it blocks a core task. Compare a focused fix, reducing scope, or delaying the affected part of the release. Make the consequence and recommendation clear to the release owner; a deadline alone does not justify accepting exclusion. Verify the fix against the relevant accessibility criteria and interaction before closing it. Describe this as your proposed approach: no matching past incident is established in the example bank.

**Priorities keep changing and several squads need you. How do you prioritise?**

Clarify the shared objective, real deadlines, user consequences, dependencies, and available capacity. Make the tradeoffs visible to the PMs and agree an ordered queue rather than accepting every request independently. When something new becomes urgent, explicitly identify what moves out. Keep enough documentation to resume deferred work and agree when to revisit priorities. The Remuner audit supports your general approach, but a specific competing-deadline episode still needs your input.
