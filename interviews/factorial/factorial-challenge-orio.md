# Preparation - Product Design Challenge

In this document, you'll find all the information you need to understand the context and format of the challenge, what we'll be evaluating, and how to prepare effectively without investing more time than necessary.

---

## The domain

In this challenge, you'll be designing for Orio, an access management platform that helps IT teams in companies with 1,000+ employees manage their software ecosystem from a single place.

Orio integrates with over 1,000 applications, including tools like Figma, Microsoft, Notion, and Slack, centralizing access and permission information across the entire stack.

IT teams use Orio to manage employee access, permissions, SSO, and the full identity lifecycle. Finance teams use it to track software contracts, renewal dates, costs, and billing information. The goal is to provide a single place to manage hundreds of applications and thousands of user accesses securely and efficiently.

> **If identity and access management is new to you,** we recommend you spend some time familiarizing yourself with the space before starting the exercise. That said, we do not expect you to arrive with specific domain knowledge, industry vocabulary, or pre-built solutions.

## The format (2h)

Two hours, each with a clear purpose.

### Hour 1: You alone

During the first hour, you'll receive an automated email with a more detailed brief that provides additional context about the problem we're trying to solve.

From here, we expect you to:

- **Develop a deep understanding of the problem**, going beyond the surface-level symptoms.
- **Explore potential directions** and form initial hypotheses about where the solution could go.
- **Capture your thinking** in whatever format works best for you, notes, sketches, diagrams, references, etc., so you can later walk us through your reasoning.

### Hour 2: You and two directors

Two of us will join you. We'll work through the problem together in two phases:

- **Framing and MVP:** we'll discuss your understanding of the problem and define together what's in your MVP and what stays for future iterations.
- **Taking it to screens:** we'll design one or two key screens together that explain your decisions and show how the flows behave.

---

## What we evaluate

- **Problem framing.** How you prioritize between user, business, and operational goals when they pull in different directions, and how you defend your prioritization.
- **Systemic thinking.** How you model a complex problem into a structure that scales: which entities and relationships you choose, which tradeoffs you make explicit, and how decisions on one axis affect the rest.
- **Craft.** How you exercise design judgment to build experiences that are not only functional but distinct, opinionated about the problem, considered in their detail, and that go a step beyond just solving the problem.

# Briefing - Product Design Challenge

In this document, you'll find the case you'll work on for the next two hours. Read it carefully, the first hour is yours to use as you consider.

---

## The problem

At Orio, we've been talking to many of our customers, and it's clear we can do a lot more to help them manage access. Here are some of the symptoms we've consistently seen:

1. **People often start without the access they need.** Days or weeks go by between someone joining the company and getting the right tools in their hands. Managers end up opening tickets manually after noticing their new hire can't do their job.
2. **Role and team changes generate huge volumes of access tickets.** Every time someone moves to a new team or shifts responsibilities, they spend their first weeks opening tickets for the tools they should already have. Most of the access tickets IT receives are accesses the person "should already have", but nobody had configured that anywhere.
3. **People who leave keep access long after they're gone.** Employees who left months ago still have active licenses and production access. Contractors who finished a project keep their seats in Slack channels and shared workspaces. Most of the time, IT only finds out by accident, often when finance flags an extra cost.
4. **The handoff between HR and IT is broken.** The HR team records every employee movement in their HRIS, but IT only finds out through emails, Slack messages, or manual tickets. A lot of that communication gets lost along the way.

---

## The challenge

Design how Orio's ecosystem should evolve to help companies address the four problems presented above. Think about what the right shape of that system is, how it behaves over time, and how every stakeholder should interact with it.

Your design should hold up across companies with different sizes, structures, and stacks. What matters is that your decisions are defensible.

## Expectations for hour 1

In the first hour, you'll work independently, so there's no need to join the call until the second hour. During this time, focus on understanding the problem and forming a clear point of view. By the time we meet, we'd love to see:

- **A clear understanding of the problem:** not the symptoms, but what's underneath them.
- **At least one direction you'd take the solution.** You don't need a fully mapped set of alternatives; we'll explore those together.
- **Notes, sketches, diagrams, or references** that help you think and help us follow your reasoning. Format is up to you.

> **You do not need to produce a polished deliverable.** What matters is that you arrive at hour two with a clear point of view and a rationale you can discuss and defend.

## Expectations for hour 2

Two of us will join you for the call at this stage.

**Think of it less as a presentation and more as a working session with future colleagues.** We'll move through it in two phases:

- **Framing and MVP:** we'll discuss your understanding of the problem and define together what's in your MVP and what stays for future iterations.
- **Taking it to screens:** we'll design one or two key screens together that explain your decisions and show how the flows behave.

Throughout the session, you should remain the primary driver of the work. We'll actively challenge assumptions, ask questions, and provide additional context when needed. Think of us as stakeholders and collaborators helping you explore the problem space, clarify ambiguities, and stress-test your decisions.

> **Don't worry about reaching a closed ending.** The goal isn't a finished design, it's a conversation that lets us see how you think.
