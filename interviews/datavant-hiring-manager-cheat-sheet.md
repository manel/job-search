# Datavant: Hiring Manager cheat sheet

**30 min · Mary Bergeron · Senior UX Designer, Provider Experience · 30 Sep 2026, 20:30 CEST**

## Positioning

- Product designer in Barcelona with more than fifteen years of experience. Analytical, calm, and hands-on.
- I work on complex B2B workflows with Product and Engineering.
- I question assumptions when the evidence points elsewhere and make the tradeoffs clear.
- **Why Datavant:** connect complex tools and help providers complete more work without support.

Use **I** for my work and **we** for shared decisions. If I lose the thread: **“The problem was… My contribution was… The outcome was…”**

## Story selector

| Prompt | Retrieval cues |
| --- | --- |
| **Research and self-service** | **Dataset Manager:** Users understood the logic in Excel but could not use SQL. We extended the Excel-like language. Customers reached a working setup in their first session. The implementation team estimated that standard setups took roughly half the previous time. |
| **Connecting separate tools** | **Dataset and Dimensions:** Creating a missing category forced users to leave the dataset. I proposed moving basic creation and editing into it. Customers reported faster setup, fewer errors, and less confusion. |
| **Proud work** | **Modeling language:** Several syntax styles made formulas hard to learn. I researched the problem and worked with Engineering on one consistent pattern. It later supported clearer documentation and an inline formula helper. |
| **Engineering pushback** | **Maker Days:** Engineering raised a capacity concern. The Head of Design and I proposed a one-week test. The team put the tokens and first Button into production. This proved the process worked, but did not prove a financial return. |
| **Leadership disagreement** | **Manager view:** I recommended fixing the workflow before a full redesign. The CEO and CTO chose the redesign. I added metric tabs and reduced clicks. Those changes received positive feedback, but the overall response was mixed. |
| **Product and Engineering** | Keep them involved from research through delivery. Agree on the problem, share evidence early, discuss tradeoffs together, and stay close during implementation. **Dataset Manager:** the PM, lead engineer, and I used the research finding to change direction from SQL to the Excel-like language. |
| **Prioritisation** | **Abax roadmap:** Missing components and variants blocked product work. We prioritised those gaps over more token work and reviewed the order each week. |
| **Mistake** | **Token file:** I deleted a file that the product still needed and caused an outage. I restored it, pushed the fix, and checked files manually. The team later added a required-file check. |

## Short answers

- **Healthcare:** Learn the users, language, regulations, and constraints. Combine internal expertise with provider research.
- **Workflow:** Map the task, decisions, permissions, and dependencies. Test risky assumptions early.
- **First weeks:** Learn the product, common workflows, user problems, and long-term direction. Then choose a focused first problem.
- **Strength:** Systems thinking. I turn inconsistencies into components, behaviours, and rules the team can maintain.
- **Development:** I am improving formal presentations in English by using clear anchors and rehearsing aloud.
- **Remuner exit:** My role ended after a company decision. I am looking for earlier design involvement in defining problems.
- **Notifications:** I have no past case. Start with the user, action, urgency, channel, owner, privacy, and escalation rules.
