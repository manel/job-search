At Abacum, I led design and customer testing for a tool that helped finance teams configure their own data.

Before that, they depended on the squad's engineers and technical configuration files. The goal was to give them more independence.

Our early direction combined a simpler interface with SQL. In testing, users could explain the transformation they needed using Excel functions, but they struggled to express it in SQL.

I took that finding to Product and Engineering. We shipped SQL-like formulas first, because they were easier to build, and then moved to the existing modeling language, which finance users already knew.

The guided workflow shipped. Our recollection was that customers could reach a working configuration in their first session. The squad's engineers estimated standard setups took roughly half the previous time. That was their estimate, not an analytics measurement.
