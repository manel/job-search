# LinkedIn job-search playbook

Last reviewed: 2026-10-02

## Purpose

Use LinkedIn to:

1. Become easier for relevant recruiters to find.
2. Discover openings and hiring activity early.
3. Identify a credible contact for strong roles.

LinkedIn is a discovery and context source. Always verify that the exact vacancy is open on the employer's official careers site or applicant-tracking system before assessing or applying.

## Targeting constraint

The current target (`context/target-roles.md`) is remote work from Spain or hybrid work in Barcelona. If this constraint has not changed, exclude on-site roles from LinkedIn preferences and searches.

Target titles:

- Senior Product Designer
- Staff Product Designer
- Lead Product Designer, individual contributor
- Principal Product Designer
- Product Designer, Design Systems

Avoid broad titles such as `UX Designer` unless deliberately widening the search. They introduce substantial agency, production-design, and lower-relevance noise.

## 1. Configure Open to Work for recruiters

Use recruiter-only visibility with:

- The target titles above
- Barcelona/Catalonia and Spain
- Remote and hybrid workplace types
- Full-time employment

LinkedIn says these preferences influence recruiter searches and job recommendations. It attempts to hide recruiter-only status from recruiters at the current employer but does not guarantee this.

Source: [LinkedIn: Let recruiters know you're open to work](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508), checked 2026-10-02.

## 2. Search hiring posts

Two or three times per week, search LinkedIn Posts rather than only the Jobs index. Example searches:

- `"Senior Product Designer" hiring Barcelona`
- `"Staff Product Designer" remote Spain`
- `"Lead Product Designer" hiring fintech`
- `"Design Systems Designer" hiring`

Select **Posts**, then **Content type: Job posts**. A hiring post can reveal the hiring manager or design leader, making focused follow-up easier.

Source: [LinkedIn: Filter and sort job-search results](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507441/filter-and-sort-job-search-results), checked 2026-10-02.

## 3. Follow priority companies

For roughly 10 to 20 companies that are genuinely attractive:

- Follow the company page.
- Follow relevant design and product leaders.
- Check the official careers page weekly.
- Use **I'm interested** where LinkedIn makes it available.

LinkedIn says eligible recruiters can see expressions of interest for up to one year. This is a visibility signal, not an application.

Source: [LinkedIn: Express interest in working for a company](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1378526), checked 2026-10-02.

## 4. Contact one relevant person per strong role

Apply first; do not delay an application while waiting for a response. Then contact one relevant person: the hiring manager, design leader, recruiter, or a genuinely known employee.

Suggested message:

> Hi [Name], I've applied for the [role] position. My background is in complex B2B workflows and design systems, and [specific product or team detail] is what caught my attention. This is the most relevant case study: [direct link]. I'd appreciate any context you're comfortable sharing about the team or scope.

A first-degree LinkedIn connection is not necessarily a real relationship. If there is no prior relationship, ask for perspective rather than a referral.

Do not message multiple employees at the same company with the same request.

## 5. Improve recruiter searchability

The current headline is already aligned:

> Senior Product Designer | Complex B2B products, data-heavy workflows & design systems | AI product design

Maintain the following elsewhere in the profile, where supported by evidence:

- Exact target titles in the About section and relevant experience
- Complex B2B products
- Data-heavy workflows
- Design systems
- Product strategy
- AI product design
- Direct links to the strongest relevant case studies in Featured
- Skills ordered around the target roles

Do not make recruiters navigate from the portfolio homepage when a direct case-study link is available.

LinkedIn says job recommendations use profile information and Open to Work preferences.

Source: [LinkedIn: Job recommendations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a512279/), checked 2026-10-02.

## 6. Avoid low-yield activity

Do not spend substantial time on:

- Mass Easy Apply submissions
- Generic `I'm looking for work` posts
- Cold referral requests
- Messaging several employees at one company
- Posting daily without useful subject matter
- Buying Premium solely for the Top Applicant label

LinkedIn says recruiters do not see a candidate's job-match level or match summary.

Source: [LinkedIn: Job-match insights](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7120158), checked 2026-10-02.

## Weekly routine

- **Daily, 10 minutes:** Review new roles. Dismiss weak matches and verify strong ones on the official company site.
- **Twice weekly, 15 minutes:** Search hiring posts using the target titles.
- **Weekly, 30 minutes:** Review priority company pages, official careers sites, and relevant design or product leaders.
- **For each strong role:** Verify the official posting, assess fit, apply promptly, and send one focused message where there is a credible recipient.

## Decision rule

Do not treat a LinkedIn listing, snippet, hiring post, or company careers index as proof that a role is open. The exact official posting is canonical.
